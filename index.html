Who We Are

A trusted partner for a once‑in‑a‑generation moment.

The NSW Government's proposed new native title policy — released January 2026 — changes everything. For the first time, Aboriginal communities can settle native title claims and receive compensation without going to court.

Compensation is paid as a lump sum into a community-controlled charitable trust, calculated to sustain your RNTBC in perpetuity. This is the moment communities have been waiting for. Getting the trust structure, investment strategy and governance right from the start will determine whether these funds last for generations — or don't.

"Communities make the best decisions for their own futures. Our job is to make sure they have the knowledge, structure and confidence to do exactly that."

Purple Wombat is Aboriginal-led and Aboriginal-owned. We bring cultural authority and genuine community knowledge together with deep expertise in trust law, investment governance, financial management and community development.

We are not connected to trustees, fund managers or legal firms who benefit from your decisions. Our only interest is yours.

Our Foundations

What we stand for — and why it matters.

01

Self-Determination

Communities know what's best for their own people and Country. The evidence is clear — when Aboriginal communities make their own decisions about their funds and futures, they out-perform externally managed approaches every time. Purple Wombat exists to ensure communities have the knowledge and capacity to exercise that self-determination effectively.

02

Intergenerational Thinking

The compensation trusts are designed to run in perpetuity — to sustain the RNTBC and fund community aspirations not just today, but for the next generation and the one after that. We help communities think in that timeframe from day one. The decisions made now about trust structure and investment policy will still be felt by your grandchildren.

03

Cultural Authority and Trust

Purple Wombat is Aboriginal-owned and led. We bring cultural authority to this work — not just technical expertise. Communities don't have to translate their world for us. We understand it. We engage in a trauma-informed, culturally appropriate way — working at the community's pace, in their language, on their terms.

NSW Government Policy 2026

The rules have changed.
Here's what it means for your community.

The NSW Government has proposed a landmark new policy for settling native title claims and compensation — without litigation, without years of court battles, without the trauma that process inflicts. For the first time, communities can settle on their own terms.

"You have rights. The new NSW policy means you don't have to go through years of costly court battles to be recognised and compensated. But the decisions you make now — about your trust, your investments, your governance — will affect your community for generations."
