Mission & Vision

Empowering communities to own, govern and grow their futures.

Purple Wombat walks alongside Aboriginal communities through every step of the NSW native title settlement process — from understanding their rights, to establishing robust trust structures, to managing compensation funds for the benefit of current and future generations.

We are Aboriginal led, Aboriginal owned, and driven by the principle that communities know best. We combine deep cultural knowledge with technical expertise in trust law, investment governance, financial management and community development.

We are guided by the same principles that underpin the NSW Government's new policy: self-determination, equity, timeliness and intergenerational thinking — because we believe no community should have to choose between getting justice now and securing their children's futures.

Our Positioning

Brand Personality

Five traits that define who we are — and how we work.

Grounded

We are connected to Country, to community, and to the reality of what people are navigating. We don't overcomplicate, we don't use jargon to impress, and we don't lose sight of the people at the centre of the work. When we say we understand the weight of this process, we mean it — not as a service line, but as a lived truth.

Warm

We are approachable and human. Communities should feel safe with us — never judged, never rushed, never talked down to. We earn trust by showing up consistently and genuinely, at the community's pace and on the community's terms. That warmth is not a performance. It is who we are.

Confident

We know our field. We are clear and direct in what we say and what we recommend. We do not hedge for the sake of being polite, and we do not back down when we know something matters to a community. Confidence in our expertise is how we serve — not to assert authority, but to give communities the strongest possible advice.

Culturally Rooted

Aboriginal identity is not a decoration for Purple Wombat — it is foundational. Our leadership, our values, our ways of working, and our relationships with communities reflect genuine cultural authority. Communities don't have to translate their world for us. We already understand it, because we come from it.

Forward-Looking

We think in generations, not in quarters. The decisions communities make about their trusts today will shape their grandchildren's lives. We carry that responsibility with seriousness and optimism — because the opportunity this new policy creates, if handled well, can sustain communities in perpetuity.

What We Stand For

Five commitments that guide everything we do.

01

Self-determination works.

The evidence is clear — when Aboriginal communities make their own decisions about their funds and futures, they out-perform externally managed approaches. Purple Wombat exists to ensure communities have the knowledge and capacity to exercise that self-determination effectively.

02

This moment won't wait.

The NSW Government's new policy is a once-in-a-generation opportunity. Getting the trust structure, investment approach and governance right from the start will determine whether compensation funds last for generations — or don't. The decisions made now matter.

03

Cultural safety is non-negotiable.

Purple Wombat engages with communities in a trauma-informed, culturally appropriate way. We work at the community's pace, in their language, on their terms. Cultural safety is not a compliance checkbox — it is the foundation everything else is built on.

04

Independent. Always.

Purple Wombat is not connected to trustees, fund managers or legal firms who benefit from your decisions. We receive no referral fees. Our only interest is the community's. When our advice might be uncomfortable for government or institutional partners, we give it anyway — because that's what communities deserve.

05

Building lasting capability.

Our goal is not to create dependency on Purple Wombat — it is to build the governance, financial and strategic capacity of each community and RNTBC so they can thrive independently. Success for us means the day communities no longer need us.

Our Ecosystem

The system Purple Wombat operates within — and the relationships that matter.

Understanding who's who in the native title process is half the battle. Purple Wombat helps communities navigate this ecosystem — knowing when to engage, who has authority, and where to push back.

StakeholderRelationship to Communities
NSW Dept of Planning, Housing & Infrastructure (DPHI) Administers the native title policy; the counterparty in negotiations. Purple Wombat helps communities engage effectively with DPHI.
NSW Attorney General Approves each compensation settlement and trust governance arrangements — an important approval milestone in the process.
National Native Title Tribunal Registers ILUAs and plays a formal role in the settlement process. Purple Wombat explains what registration means and what it locks in.
Federal Court of Australia Issues consent determinations. The Court's role is largely administrative under the new policy — it ratifies what communities and the Government have agreed through negotiation.
NTSCorp (NSW Native Title Services) The representative body for native title groups in NSW. A key referral and collaboration partner for Purple Wombat.
Professional Trustees Appointed initially to administer community trusts. Purple Wombat supports communities in selecting, briefing and overseeing professional trustees — and in managing the transition to community control.
ACNC Registers and regulates the charitable trusts. Purple Wombat assists with registration and ongoing compliance requirements.
NSW Land Management Agencies National Parks, Crown Lands, Forestry, water agencies — parties to ILUAs. Purple Wombat helps communities understand what each agency's involvement means for their rights and Country.
Brand Guardrails

Who we are not — and why that matters.

These are not aspirations. They are firm commitments. Every piece of Purple Wombat's work is held against these principles before it goes to community.

We Are Not Tokenistic

Aboriginal cultural identity must be authentic and central — not decorative. We don't use the word 'reconciliation' where it means nothing, and we don't claim cultural authority we haven't earned. This work is Aboriginal-led because it has to be — not because it looks good.

We Are Not Preachy

We don't lecture communities about their rights. We don't lead with colonisation in contexts where it's not relevant. We demonstrate our values through action and advice — not through statements. Communities know their history. Our job is to help them build their future.

We Are Not Fragile or Apologetic

Purple Wombat knows what it stands for. We don't hedge our mission, soften our position to make institutional partners comfortable, or understate the significance of the work communities are facing. When confident, clear advice is what's needed, that's what we give.

We Are Not Corporate or Cold

We don't hide behind legal or financial jargon, treat communities as beneficiaries rather than rights-holders, or produce documents that look like bank reports. Every piece of communication is accessible, warm and written for people — not for files.
