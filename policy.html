NSW Government Policy · January 2026

A new way to
settle native title.
Without going to court.

For the first time, NSW Aboriginal communities can receive native title compensation through negotiation rather than litigation — as a lump sum, held in a community-controlled trust, designed to sustain your RNTBC in perpetuity.

Why a New Approach

The old system wasn't working.

Until now, there has been no settled policy in NSW to guide the Government in managing native title claims and compensation consistently. This made it almost impossible to resolve claims in a timely and equitable manner.

Litigation placed enormous legal, financial and emotional burden on native title groups — with uncertain outcomes. There were often long delays between a native title determination and any compensation claim, meaning communities had their rights recognised on paper but no resources to actually exercise them.

Some claims took so long to finalise that community members never saw the outcomes. That changes now.

"The new policy enables compensation to be paid to native title holders more quickly — and without the trauma and cost of court."

The new policy is available to:
  • Native title groups with existing consent determinations who have not yet settled compensation
  • Native title groups who lodge (or have already lodged) a native title claim meeting the credible evidence standard
  • RNTBCs seeking to negotiate a whole-of-government ILUA
Policy Foundations

Four principles that guide the new approach.

The NSW Government's proposed policy is designed to align with international best practice and support native title negotiations to achieve genuine cultural, social, economic and environmental outcomes.

Self-Determination

Each native title group has the right to determine their own aspirations. The policy acknowledges that each group is unique and that negotiation cannot be 'one-size-fits-all'. It explicitly recognises the historical dispossession of Aboriginal people from land and culture, and seeks to redress that.

Equity

A consistent state-wide approach that considers the needs of each group to operate a sustainable RNTBC and fulfil aspirations for generations to come. Compensation is calculated using the same method for all claim groups — giving every community the same foundation to build on.

Timeliness

Greater certainty and better outcomes with a shorter, less adversarial process to resolve compensation — while avoiding the costs and psychological impacts of litigation. No more waiting years for recognition that should have come decades ago.

Cost-Effectiveness

The new policy avoids the potentially unfavourable and variable outcomes of litigation, generating consistent income from compensation held in trust and supporting native title groups to establish commercial and employment opportunities over time.

The Three Elements

Every settlement under the new policy has three parts — agreed at the same time.

The Government's aim is to settle claims by agreeing all three elements concurrently. For groups who already have a consent determination, the aim is to settle compensation and an ILUA together. This is an opt-in policy — communities always retain the option to litigate instead.

01
Element One

Consent Determination

A consent determination is a legally binding agreement reached through mediation and given effect by the Federal Court. It states what native title rights are recognised, the lands and/or waters where they exist, and who the members of the group are that hold those rights.

The Government is committed to supporting native title groups to achieve recognition through negotiation rather than litigation — and this approach continues under the new policy. This is about getting formal recognition of rights that have always existed.

A consent determination confirms:
  • What native title rights are recognised
  • The lands and/or waters where those rights exist
  • Who holds those rights as members of the native title group
02
Element Two

Compensation, Held in a Community-Controlled Trust

Compensation is paid as a lump sum into a Trust established by the RNTBC. The Trust generates interest and income, ensuring the RNTBC is financially sustainable in perpetuity — without needing to rely on government grants or external funding.

The Government will have no role in how your community chooses to spend the trust's income. The calculation is designed to meet the costs of running your RNTBC and funding community aspirations — but spending decisions belong entirely to your community.

How Compensation Is Calculated

The calculation uses a consistent method for all claim groups — not based on trying to value land or cultural loss (which courts struggle to assess), but on what it will actually cost to run a sustainable RNTBC and fund your community's aspirations over an intergenerational timeline.

What's Included in the Calculation Purpose
Annual costs of running an RNTBC (staffing, office, operations, governance) Ensures the RNTBC can operate effectively from day one
Joint management governance of national parks in the native title holders' Country Covers the RNTBC's role as co-manager of Country
Costs of funding a ranger program Supports ongoing connection to and management of Country
Annual aspirations budget (economic, environmental, social, cultural) Funds community programs, land acquisition, culture — as the community chooses

Economic assumptions including inflation and investment returns are applied to calculate the total lump sum required to sustain these costs in perpetuity. The Government's model assumes a blended 70% conservative / 30% growth investment approach.

03
Element Three

Indigenous Land Use Agreement (ILUA)

An ILUA is a legally binding agreement between a native title group and the Government (and other parties) about an area of land and/or water where native title exists. It is registered with the National Native Title Tribunal and is binding on all members of the native title group.

Under the proposed policy, the whole-of-government ILUA covers two things: agreed restrictions on how native title rights are exercised (to allow land management agencies to do their jobs), and agreed benefits and opportunities for native title holders in exchange for those restrictions.

What an ILUA can secure for your community:
  • Transfer of Crown land to the RNTBC
  • Cultural heritage programs and protections
  • Employment and commercial opportunities
  • Access to land for cultural and economic purposes
  • A future acts regime — procedures for managing proposed activities that may affect native title
  • Preferential contracting arrangements with government agencies
The Trust Arrangement

A trust designed to sustain your community — in perpetuity.

"The Government recognises that when Aboriginal communities make their own decisions about how their funds are spent, they consistently out-perform external decision-makers — such as government agencies — in delivering positive outcomes for their community."
NSW Government Discussion Paper, 2026
1
Lump Sum Paid

The NSW Government pays compensation as a single lump sum into the community's charitable trust upon settlement.

2
Trust Invests

The trust invests the funds according to the community's Investment Policy Statement — designed to grow at or above inflation in perpetuity.

3
Income Flows to Community

Trust income and distributions flow to community programs, services, cultural activities and economic opportunities — as your community decides.

Initially managed by a professional trustee, the trust transitions to RNTBC-appointed trustee control as capacity is built. Purple Wombat supports communities through every stage of that transition.

Common Questions

What communities ask us most.

Do we have to opt in to this policy?

No. The new policy is entirely opt-in. Your community can always choose to decline the Government's offer and litigate compensation through the Federal Court instead. The policy creates an alternative pathway, not an obligation. Purple Wombat can help you understand the full implications of both options before you decide.

If we accept, can we still go to court later?

If you accept a compensation settlement under the policy, you agree not to lodge a further compensation claim in the Federal Court in relation to the same land — except for limited categories of future acts. This is why understanding the implications fully before accepting is so important. Purple Wombat provides independent review of compensation offers.

Who controls how the trust money is spent?

Your community. The Government has no role in how trust income is spent once it is in the trust. A Trust Deed sets out governance principles and trustee accountability — but spending decisions belong entirely to the native title holders and the RNTBC. Purple Wombat helps communities develop distribution policies that reflect their own aspirations and priorities.

What if we think the compensation calculation is wrong?

Communities have the right to scrutinise and question how their compensation has been calculated. Purple Wombat assists communities to understand the calculation — including examining the inputs (claim size, group size, aspirations budget) — and supports independent assessment processes where disputes arise.

We already have a consent determination. Does this policy apply to us?

Yes. The policy is explicitly available to native title groups who have an existing consent determination but have not yet settled compensation. If your RNTBC has been recognised but hasn't received compensation, the new policy creates a path to getting that settled — and getting funds into a trust — significantly faster than going to court.
